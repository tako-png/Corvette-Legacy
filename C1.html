First generation (C1; 1953–1962)

The Chevrolet Corvette was born in 1953, when General Motors unveiled a two-seat fiberglass-bodied sports car at the GM Motorama in New York. Just 300 hand-built units rolled off the line that first year, all Polo White with red interiors, each powered by a modest 150-horsepower "Blue Flame" inline-six paired to a two-speed automatic transmission.


  • Body: First mass-produced car with a fiberglass body
  • Design: Two-seat convertible, wraparound windshield (1953–1962), iconic "toothy" front grille
  • Engine options: Inline-six "Blue Flame" (1953–55), later small-block V8s up to 360 hp
  • Fuel injection: Available starting 1957 — first for an American production car
  • Transmission: 2-speed Powerglide automatic or optional 4-speed manual (from 1957)
  • Headlights: Dual headlights early on, switched to quad headlights in 1958
  • Styling cues: Cove side panels (1956+), faux hood louvers, chrome trim, round taillights
  • Interior: Simple analog gauges, bench-style bucket seats, minimal luxury features
  • Top speed: Up to ~130 mph in later fuel-injected V8 models
  • Production numbers: Roughly 69,000 units built over the generation
