Second generation (C2; 1963–1967)

The Corvette entered a bold new era in 1963 with the introduction of the "Sting Ray," an all-new design inspired by the Mako Shark concept car. It marked the first time the Corvette was offered as a coupe, and the 1963 model's split rear window remains one of the most iconic (and short-lived) design details in Corvette history — dropped after just one year.


  • Nickname: The "Sting Ray" generation
  • Design: All-new body with sharp, angular lines inspired by the Mako Shark concept car
  • Split window: 1963 Coupe featured a distinctive rear window split down the middle (one year only)
  • Body styles: First Corvette offered as both a coupe and a convertible
  • Independent rear suspension: New for 1963 — a major handling upgrade over the C1's solid rear axle
  • Hidden headlights: Introduced pop-up/rotating headlight covers for a cleaner front end
  • Engine options: Small-block and big-block V8s, up to 435 hp (427 cu in, 1966–67)
  • Transmission: 3-speed or 4-speed manual, 2-speed Powerglide automatic
  • Top speed: Around 140+ mph in top big-block trims
  • Styling cues: Sculpted body creases, dual round taillights, side vents ("gills") on early models
  • Production numbers: Roughly 118,000 units built over the generation
