Third generation (C3; 1968–1982)

The Corvette entered its longest-running generation in 1968 with a radical new "Coke bottle" design inspired by the Mako Shark II concept. Over its 15-year run, the C3 saw major shifts — from powerful big-block muscle car years in the late '60s and early '70s to leaner, emissions-focused models through the late '70s and early '80s as the automotive industry changed.


  • Design: Sweeping "Coke bottle" body shape with a long hood and short rear deck
  • Body styles: Coupe (with removable T-top roof panels from 1968) and convertible
  • Chrome bumpers: Featured through 1972, replaced by body-color urethane bumpers starting in 1973 for new safety regulations
  • Engine options: Small-block and big-block V8s, up to 435 hp in early years (1969 L88); power dropped significantly through the mid-70s due to emissions standards
  • Convertible discontinued: Dropped after 1975, not offered again until the C4 generation
  • "Stingray" name: Used as one word from 1969–1976 (dropped, then later revived)
  • Interior updates: More comfort-focused over time, including improved dash design and available leather
  • Anniversary edition: Silver Anniversary model in 1978, including a special Indy Pace Car replica
  • Production numbers: Over 542,000 units built — the highest of any Corvette generation
